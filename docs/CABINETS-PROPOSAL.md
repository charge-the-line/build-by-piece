# Kitchen cabinets: approved proposal

**Status: approved by Max with the defaults for every question, and stage 1 is built.** The rules now live in [RULES.md](RULES.md), section K. Max's answer to the questions was "just use test data", so the test cases are standard made-up sizes, not a real kitchen. This page is kept as the record of what was proposed; stages 2 and 3 are not started.

## The idea

Add four kitchen cabinets to the app, built the same way as the vanity: a plywood box, a solid oak face frame, pocket screws and glue, and a separate 2x4 platform under the base cabinets.

| Cabinet | What it is |
|---|---|
| Base cabinet | A drawer on top with a door below, and a shelf inside |
| Drawer base | A stack of drawers |
| Sink base | Doors only, with a fixed panel on top where a drawer would be, and room for the sink and pipes |
| Upper cabinet | Hangs on the wall. Doors and shelves, no drawers, no platform |

Almost every rule the vanity uses carries over unchanged: the box (B), the face frame (F), opening sizes (L), doors and drawer fronts (D), drawer boxes (X), the platform (P), joints (J), cutting (C) and tools (T). This proposal covers only what is new.

## Three stages

I suggest building this in three stages, each reviewed before the next.

1. **One cabinet at a time.** She picks a cabinet type from pictures, enters three numbers, and gets the full plan, exactly as with the vanity.
2. **A run of cabinets along one wall.** Several cabinets in one project, left to right, with one shared platform, one shopping list, and cutting diagrams that share sheets between cabinets.
3. **A room.** Walls, windows, appliances and plumbing. This is where photos or AI suggestions would come in, if at all.

The rest of this page is stage 1.

## K1. Three numbers, with a sensible start

She still enters width, depth and height, and they still mean what they mean for the vanity (rule G2). Each cabinet type starts with standard numbers already filled in, so most of the time she only changes the width.

| Cabinet | Depth (wall to front of frame) | Height (the box) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base, drawer base, sink base | 24" | 31" | With the 3 1/2" platform that is 34 1/2", which is 36" with a standard 1 1/2" countertop |
| Upper | 12" | 30" | The most common upper size |

## K2. Base cabinets sit on the same platform as the vanity

The box sits on a separate 2x4 platform with an oak toe kick (rules P1 to P5). Why: she has already built one, it is easy to level, and the sides stay simple rectangles. The alternative is a toe kick notched out of the side panels, which is how shop-built cabinets are usually made.

## K3. A drawer over a door

Today each space in the frame holds either a door or a column of drawers. A base cabinet needs both in one space: a drawer on top and a door below.

- Any space can hold a stack of openings, top to bottom, each one a drawer or a door.
- The drawer front on top is one board high (rule L4), a piece of frame goes under it (rule F5), and the door takes the rest of the height (rule L3).
- The drawer box and its slides follow rules X1 to X6 unchanged.

## K4. One door or two

A wide door sags and swings out a long way.

- If an opening would be more than 18" wide, it is split into two spaces with a vertical frame piece between them (rule F4), each with its own door.
- The 18" is a default she can change.
- Each space gets its own drawer above its own door, so a wide base cabinet has two drawers over two doors.

The alternative is one wide drawer across both doors. It looks cleaner, but it does not fit how the frame is divided, and a wide drawer needs a stiffer bottom. I have left it out.

## K5. Shelves

- A base cabinet gets one shelf, halfway up the space behind the door.
- An upper cabinet gets enough shelves that no gap is more than 14" (a default): two shelves in a 30" upper.
- Shelf length = the clear width between the panels on either side. Shelf depth = box depth minus the back, less 1" at the front on a base cabinet so the door closes.
- Shelves are fixed with pocket screws from underneath. Why: no extra tool and no measuring of hole positions. The alternative is adjustable shelves on pins, which needs a shelf-pin jig.

## K6. Sink base

- Doors only, sized by rule K4.
- Where the drawer would be, a fixed panel the same size as a drawer front is screwed on from behind. There is no drawer box and no slides.
- No shelf.
- The back panel stays, with the pipe openings cut on site (step A8). The alternative is no back at all, only mounting strips.
- A check warns if the cabinet is narrower than 30", which is tight for most sinks.

## K7. Upper cabinet

- No platform and no toe kick.
- The box has a top panel as well as a bottom. Both fit between the sides, like the vanity's bottom (rule B3).
- A 1/4" back fits between the sides (rule B4), with a mounting strip across the top and another across the bottom, inside the box (rule B7).
- The underside is seen, so the bottom panel has its good face down and no pocket holes showing (rule J2): they go on the top face of the bottom panel, inside the cabinet.
- Hanging it is a new step: screw a straight board to the wall as a temporary ledge, rest the cabinet on it, and screw through both mounting strips into studs. The app says this is a two-person job.

## K8. Part numbers

Ashley asked for a cabinet number and a part letter: C01-A, C01-B. One cabinet has more than 26 parts, so letters run out.

I suggest C01-01, C01-02 and so on: the cabinet number, then the part number. A single-cabinet project could drop the prefix and keep P01 as now.

## What changes in the app

- The "Start something new" screen offers the four kitchen cabinets, each with a picture.
- The Size screen shows the right picture of what the three numbers include for that cabinet (an upper has no platform).
- Parts, Cutting, Shop and Build work as now, with new steps where needed: fit a shelf, fix the sink panel, hang the upper cabinet.
- Tests: each cabinet type gets a worked example, the same checks that every piece is placed once without overlapping, and the layout checks.

## Questions

2. **Platform.** Kitchen base cabinets on the same separate 2x4 platform as the vanity (K2)? Or a toe kick notched into the sides?
3. **Starting sizes.** 24" deep and a 31" box for base cabinets; 12" deep and 30" high for uppers (K1). Are Ashley's uppers a different height (36" and 42" are also common)?
4. **Wide base cabinets.** Two drawers over two doors (K4), or one wide drawer over two doors?
5. **Widest single door.** 18" before it splits into two (K4). OK?
6. **Shelves.** Fixed with pocket screws (K5), or adjustable on pins, which means buying a shelf-pin jig?
7. **Sink base back.** Keep the back panel and cut openings (K6), or leave the back open?
8. **Part numbers.** C01-01 (K8), or letters as Ashley wrote (C01-A, then C01-AA after Z)?
9. **A real kitchen to test against.** The vanity had 60" × 21" × 30". Is there a wall, or one cabinet, in Ashley's kitchen with real sizes I should use as the test case for each type?
